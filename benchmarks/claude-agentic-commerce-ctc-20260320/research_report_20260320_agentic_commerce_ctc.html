Executive Summary

Agentic commerce — where AI agents autonomously discover, compare, negotiate, and transact on behalf of consumers — represents the largest structural shift in retail since the internet. McKinsey estimates the global opportunity at $3–5 trillion in orchestrated commerce by 2030. During Cyber Week 2025, one in five orders already involved an AI agent. The transition is not hypothetical.

Canadian Tire Corporation is uniquely positioned to win this transition — and uniquely exposed to losing it. The company's MOSaiC platform (scaling across all three major banners in 2026), its Triangle Rewards network (17M+ members, Canada's largest retail loyalty program), its five distinct banners spanning hardware, sporting goods, apparel, outdoor, and automotive, and its Canadian Tire Bank give it an asset combination that no Canadian competitor can replicate.

This report delivers a comprehensive playbook: the six-layer technology architecture; ten prioritized use cases; the open vs. closed ecosystem strategic choice; and a four-phase roadmap from data foundation (2026) through autonomous multi-step commerce (2028+).

"Triangle Rewards is not a loyalty program. It is an intelligence layer — the only permissioned, first-party, cross-banner customer data asset in Canada with the breadth and depth to power genuinely differentiated agentic commerce. The organizations that win in agentic commerce are those with privileged data. CTC has it."

Layer 1: LLM Backbone

For CTC, the LLM choice is partly determined by the existing Microsoft Azure partnership. Azure OpenAI Service (GPT-4o) is the path of least resistance — it runs inside Azure's compliance boundary, shares governance infrastructure with MOSaiC, and benefits from Microsoft's retail AI acceleration programs [8]. A mixed model approach is standard enterprise architecture: Azure OpenAI for customer-facing agents, Claude for document analysis, Gemini for visual search and external agent exposure.

Layer 2: Retrieval and Knowledge (Agentic RAG)

For a retailer with CTC's scale (100,000+ SKUs across five banners), RAG requires a vector database for semantic search — "durable waterproof jacket for camping in cold weather" retrieves relevant products even when those exact words don't appear in product descriptions. Azure AI Search (native to CTC's existing Microsoft stack) supports hybrid vector + keyword search with reranking. The MOSaiC occasion taxonomy — 1,000+ life occasions — should be embedded as semantic context in the retrieval layer, so product retrieval is occasion-aware, not just query-aware [11].

Layer 4: The Tool Layer — What the Agent Can Do

An agent without tools is a chatbot. The tool layer defines what the agent can act on. The minimum viable tool set for a CTC agentic system:

Layer 5: Orchestration — Multi-Agent Architecture for CTC

CTC should architect a hierarchy of specialist agents rather than a single monolithic agent. This mirrors Walmart's documented strategy: "Agents work best when deployed for highly specific tasks, to produce outputs that can then be stitched together to orchestrate and solve complex workflows" [4].

Layer 6: Protocol Layer — Getting Found by External Agents

Three protocols are converging as the global standard for agent-commerce interoperability:

  • Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP): Google/Walmart co-developed open standard for AI agent discovery and purchase routing. Adobe and Salesforce committed in early 2026. Non-compliant retailers are invisible to Google Gemini shopping queries [10].
  • Agentic Commerce Protocol (ACP): Agent-to-merchant communication covering authentication, intent signaling, and transaction authorization across any ACP-compliant agent.
  • Visa Trusted Agent Protocol (TAP): Cryptographically signed HTTP messages that enable merchants to verify a legitimate AI agent transaction from a verified customer — preventing bot fraud while enabling autonomous purchases. Already in production with hundreds of completed transactions [12].
Section 3

CTC's Existing AI Foundation

MOSaiC: Micro-Occasions in Retail Intelligence

CTC's most strategically significant technology asset — and most underpublicized — is MOSaiC. Built on Microsoft Azure, MOSaiC synthesizes Triangle Rewards loyalty data, internal sales data, and external contextual signals (weather patterns, seasonality, local events, holidays) to identify customer life occasions at scale [8].

MOSaiC Pilot Results (2025)

During pilot testing, MOSaiC identified more than 1,000 distinct life occasions where CTC is uniquely positioned to serve customers — from "spring-thaw flooding" to "back-to-school move-ins" to "new fitness routines." Scaling to Canadian Tire, Mark's, and SportChek in 2026. Microsoft providing cloud infrastructure, AI model capabilities, and engineering collaboration.

In its current form, MOSaiC outputs analytics that inform marketing and merchandising teams. The transformation to agentic commerce means routing MOSaiC's occasion intelligence directly into customer-facing AI agents that act on those insights without human intermediation. The analytical insight becomes an agentic action.

Triangle Rewards: The Intelligence Moat

17 million Triangle Rewards members in Canada (population: 40 million) represents extraordinary market penetration. More importantly, it captures cross-banner purchasing behavior — a member's Triangle account knows they shop at Canadian Tire for automotive, SportChek for hockey, and Mark's for workwear. This cross-category view is irreplaceable: no competitor can buy this data or build it quickly.

The Triangle Rewards API Readiness Gap

Eagle Eye research shows most loyalty programs fail the agentic readiness test: they lack machine-readable rules, sub-second response times, and real-time balance verification [14]. Triangle Rewards must be assessed against these criteria. If offer adjudication currently runs in batch (hours), it must be re-platformed to real-time API (sub-100ms) before agentic deployment. This is the critical path item for every use case in this playbook.

Section 6

The Open vs. Closed Ecosystem Strategic Choice

The most consequential strategic decision for CTC's agentic commerce program is a governance question, not a technology question: how open should CTC be to external AI agents accessing its systems?

Option A

Closed Ecosystem

Proprietary agentic stack only. External agents (ChatGPT, Gemini) cannot access CTC catalog or checkout. All agentic traffic flows through CTC-owned interfaces.

Protects customer relationship and data. Risks invisibility as external agents dominate discovery.

Exemplar: Amazon (Rufus, Alexa+, Shop Direct — fully closed)
Option B

Open Ecosystem

UCP/ACP-compliant catalog and checkout. External agents route orders to CTC without restriction. Maximum reach, maximum discoverability.

Grows GMV from external traffic. Risks commoditization if loyalty data is also exposed.

Exemplar: Early Walmart (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity all route to Walmart)
Recommended for CTC

Layered Hybrid

Tier 1 — Proprietary: Triangle Rewards intelligence layer — never exposed to third parties. CTC owns personalization.

Tier 2 — Controlled Open: UCP/ACP catalog + inventory + pricing for external agent discovery and routing.

Tier 3 — Partner Deep: Microsoft Copilot + select partners get richer occasion context in exchange for preferential placement.

Captures external reach while protecting the data moat

CTC's revenue profile (retail margins, not advertising revenue) aligns with Walmart rather than Amazon. The disintermediation risk — being invisible to ChatGPT/Gemini shopping queries — is greater than the commoditization risk for CTC's specific category footprint. The layered hybrid protects the Triangle Rewards intelligence layer while capturing external agent traffic.

Section 9

Risk, Governance, and Canadian Regulatory Context

Technical Risk: Hallucination in Safety-Critical Recommendations

An agent that confidently recommends an incompatible part (wrong brake pads for a vehicle model) creates customer safety risk and legal liability. Mitigation: strict retrieval-grounded responses only, compatibility validation APIs, and mandatory "please verify with an associate" disclaimers for safety-critical automotive parts.

Competition Act Exposure: Price Display and AI-Generated Promotions

Canada's Competition Act Section 52 prohibits representations that are false or misleading in a material respect — courts assess the "general impression" conveyed, not literal wording. An agent stating a stale-cached price, expired promotion, or hallucinated discount creates a potential Competition Act violation. Penalties: up to $10M or 3% of worldwide gross revenue. AI hallucinations in e-commerce cost businesses an estimated $67.4 billion globally in 2024. Mitigation: all prices must come from real-time pricing APIs; never from model memory; mandatory price verification before any agent-initiated transaction; full audit logs of every price quoted by an agent. [43][44]

Regulatory Status: PIPEDA (in force), Bill C-27/AIDA (dead), Quebec Law 25 (in force)

Critical correction: Bill C-27/AIDA died on the order paper in January 2025 when Parliament was prorogued ahead of the April 2025 federal election. Canada remains governed by PIPEDA. However, the OPC has confirmed that PIPEDA's accountability and consent principles apply to agentic AI today. Quebec Law 25 (in force) requires explicit consent for automated profiling for all CTC's significant Quebec operations. CTC should design to the CPPA/AIDA standard regardless — it represents the direction of future legislation and represents best practice for PIPEDA compliance now. [45]

Gartner Warning: 40%+ of Agentic AI Projects Will Be Cancelled by 2027

Gartner projects that more than 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by 2027 due to inadequate risk controls. 80% of organizations have already encountered risky behavior from AI agents; 95% of executives experienced negative consequences from enterprise AI. The organizational readiness and governance dimension — not the technology — is the primary failure mode. CTC must invest in AI governance infrastructure at the same level as technology investment.

The Klarna Warning: Don't Over-Automate

Klarna's AI assistant (celebrated as 700 FTE equivalent) reversed course in 2025 and reinstated human agents because customers needed the human option. CTC must design hybrid escalation from day one. Human agents receiving full agent-conversation context — not a reset — are the design requirement.

Synthesis

Five Strategic Imperatives for CTC

The Five Imperatives

  • Evolve MOSaiC from analytics platform to agentic engine. The 1,000+ occasions already identified are raw material for proactive agentic commerce. The transformation — connecting MOSaiC's output to customer-facing agent actions without human intermediation — is the highest-leverage technical project CTC can undertake in 2026.
  • Triangle Rewards must be API-first and machine-readable by 2027. In the agentic era, loyalty programs that cannot be queried at sub-100ms latency are bypassed. Triangle's 17 million members are CTC's largest asset — but only if the platform is ready for agent-mediated commerce.
  • The Automotive Service Agent is CTC's most defensible agentic differentiator. No AI platform can book a tire change at a Canadian Tire Auto Centre. Build this first, build it best. It is the use case that proves agentic commerce's value, creates habit, and is impossible for Amazon or Walmart to replicate.
  • Adopt the Walmart playbook, not the Amazon playbook. CTC's revenue profile favors openness to external agent protocols. Implement UCP/ACP compliance to prevent disintermediation. Keep the Triangle Rewards intelligence layer proprietary. The product catalog, inventory, and basic checkout become machine-readable.
  • Build governance before agents. Canadian regulatory risk (PIPEDA, Law 25, forthcoming AIDA) and customer trust require consent architecture, human escalation paths, auditability, and bias monitoring from Phase 0. The cost of retrofitting compliance is vastly higher than building it in.

The Compounding Advantage

CTC's strategic position improves as agentic commerce matures. In Phase 1, any retailer with a good chatbot competes on roughly equal footing. By Phase 3, retailers with the richest first-party data, the most complete customer profiles, and the most occasion-aware intelligence layers are the structural winners. Triangle Rewards + MOSaiC + Canadian Tire Bank create a compounding flywheel that is very difficult to replicate after the fact. The imperative is to get started.
